About

I have spent twenty years building systems where software meets hardware. I started on multifunction printer software and semiconductor factory automation in Bangalore, moved to Singapore to build embedded software for Rohde & Schwarz handheld spectrum analyzers, and joined SLB in 2015.

At SLB I led the software architecture for a new generation of flow computers for the VxSpectra multiphase flow meter. We moved the software into containers so it could follow the product across processor families instead of being rewritten for each one. Each later hardware migration took weeks rather than months. I also brought augmented reality into our Singapore engineering center, which we used for remote client inspections of equipment.

Since 2024 I have led control software for hydraulic fracturing pump fleets in Sugar Land. Alongside that work, I am testing how well small language models answer questions from real equipment manuals on hardware with no network. I write about what I learn in trade publications and IEEE venues, and review papers for IEEE and Elsevier journals.

Long bio · 111 words

Sathappan Alagusundaram is a systems architect and software team lead at SLB in Sugar Land, Texas. Over twenty years he has built connected systems and products at Toshiba, Novellus Systems, Rohde & Schwarz and SLB, in India, Singapore and the United States. At SLB he led the containerized software architecture for the VxSpectra flow computers and introduced augmented reality for remote equipment inspection at the company's Singapore technology center. He now works on control software for hydraulic fracturing pump fleets. He writes for trade and IEEE publications, reviews for IEEE and Elsevier journals, and is an IEEE Senior Member. He holds a B.Tech in electronics and communication engineering from Pondicherry University.
